# Project types and templates

Source: `src/editor/projects/ProjectStore.*`, `src/editor/ProjectInfo.h`, `src/editor/templates/TemplateStore.*`, `templates/`.

## project.json

```json
{ "name": "Sample 2D Platformer", "dimension": "2d", "vr": false,
  "created": "...", "last_opened": "...", "plugins": "..." }
```

- **`dimension`** is `"2d"` or `"3d"`. 2D claims open the 2D editor and 3D claims open the 3D editor. A file with no `dimension` loads as 2D.
- **`vr`** turns a 3D project into a VR project. The legacy value `"dimension": "vr"` loads as 3D with VR on. You can flip VR in the create flow or in **Claim Settings**.
- **`plugins`** is the list of plugins enabled for this project (see below).

Each project is a folder under `projects_root` (default `./TombStoneProjects/`). It holds `project.json`, `scene.json`, `assets/`, `scripts/` and optionally `ui/`.

## Claim templates

**+ New Claim...** opens the template picker (the **Break Ground** dialog). You can filter by All, 2D, 3D, VR or Mine, or search by name, tag or plugin. Cards are grouped:

1. **Empty**: clean starting points with no sample content and no optional plugins.
2. **Samples**: playable sample claims to learn from.
3. **Mine**: your own templates from **Save as Template**.

Each card shows an engine-rendered thumbnail, a description, its dimension and VR badges, and its plugins.

Each template is `templates/<id>/template.json` plus a baked `project/` folder. That folder is copied when you create a claim, and `project.json` is then stamped with the name you picked.

### Empty templates

| id | Dimension | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| `empty-2d` | 2D | A camera, one empty tilemap layer (32 px tiles) and the 2D lighting off. Nothing else. |
| `empty-2d-topdown` | 2D | A top-down player (Player Controller, 4-way move) with `scripts/player.lua`, a follow camera and an empty tilemap. |
| `empty-2d-platformer` | 2D | A player with gravity and jump (Player Controller in **Platformer** mode) and `scripts/player.lua`. Also two rows of solid Stone ground tiles and a follow camera. |
| `empty-3d` | 3D | A camera entity (the Play view), a directional sun, a daylight sky and a ground plane. No props. |
| `empty-3d-fps` | 3D | An FPS controller (Player, **First person** view) with its eye camera as a child, `scripts/player.lua`, a sun, sky and ground plane. |
| `empty-3d-tps` | 3D | A capsule character controller (Player, **Third person**) with the orbit camera and `scripts/player.lua`, plus a sun, sky and ground plane. |
| `empty-vr` | 3D + VR | A VR rig with hands (`assets/hand.gltf`), teleport plus 45° snap turn and `scripts/player.lua`, plus a sun, sky and ground plane. Without a headset, Play rides the rig flat on the desktop. |
| `truly-blank` | 2D or 3D | No entities at all. Pick 2D or 3D with the toggle next to **Break Ground**. |

Every empty template includes a `README.md` (what is in it, how to play it, next steps) and a `project.json` whose `"plugins"` holds only the physics / movement plugins its player needs (none for Empty 2D, Empty 3D and Truly Blank), so no optional plugins (not even `ts.posse`) are on. Turn plugins on later in the **Trading Post**. The engine has no input-action mapping yet, so the controls are the engine defaults: WASD, the arrow keys or the left stick to move, and Space, E or pad A (the action button) to jump in the platformer. That is why there is no input-actions file. All of the content, including the hand model and thumbnails, is generated by the engine and is CC0.

### template.json

Fields: `id`, `name`, `dimension`, `vr`, `description`, `plugins`, `tags`, `order`, `thumbnail`, `project`, and an optional `generator`. If the `project/` folder is missing, the generator's built-in writer is used instead.

These optional fields go with the groups:

- **`group`** is `"empty"` or `"samples"` (the default). User templates always show under Mine.
- **`alt_dimension`**, **`alt_project`** and **`alt_generator`** give one card a second dimension. `truly-blank` uses them to ship `project/` (2D) and `project-3d/` (3D), and the picker's 2D/3D toggle chooses between them.

Templates are searched in this order:

1. `$TS_TEMPLATES_PATH`
2. `<exe>/templates`
3. The source tree

**Save as Template** writes the current claim into `<projects_root>/.templates/`, where it shows under **Mine**.

### Regenerating and testing

- `ts_admin --bake-templates <dir>` regenerates the baked `project/` folders from the generators.
- `ts_admin --bake-thumbnails <dir>` re-renders every `thumbnail.png` with the engine. 2D thumbnails use the runtime draw list with the claim's plugins loaded. 3D thumbnails use the GL renderer and need a display or Xvfb.
- `ts_admin --templates-shot <png>` saves a screenshot of the New Claim dialog. It uses a temporary projects root and never writes `imgui.ini`.
- `ts_admin --smoke-templates` checks the groups, the thumbnails and that the empty templates have no optional plugins (only the physics / movement plugins their player needs). It then creates a claim from **every** template (both dimensions of `truly-blank`) in temp folders, opens it in its editor and runs Play headless for a few frames. Checks include:
  - nothing gets seeded;
  - the top-down player walks;
  - the platformer player lands on the ground and jumps;
  - the FPS camera sits at eye height and looks up and down;
  - the third-person camera orbits behind the player;
  - mouse look (`--smoke-mouselook`, also run here): synthetic mouse deltas turn the FPS body and tilt the eyes, pitch stops at ±89°, invert Y and sensitivity apply, smoothing eases, the right stick turns, Keys look ignores the mouse; the third-person mouse orbit, its pitch clamp, scroll zoom and the pull-in in front of a wall; the top-down face-mouse angle; the `scene.json` round trips; and the Empty templates ship with it on;
  - VR snap turn and teleport work in the simulator.

## Empty-template features in the engine

- **2D Player Controller: Platformer mode.** In the inspector, Movement is **Top-down** or **Platformer**. Platformer walks on the horizontal axis and falls with `gravity` (px/s², default 1400). It jumps with `jump_speed` (px/s, default 560) when grounded: press W, Up or the action button (Space / E / pad A). It collides with solid tiles and colliders. In `scene.json` this is `"mode": "platformer"`, plus `gravity` and `jump_speed`, all optional.
- **2D empty scenes play.** A scene with no entities enters Play (an empty ride) instead of failing.
- **3D Player: First person view.** The Player inspector's **View** is **Third person** (orbit camera, default) or **First person**. First person puts the camera at `eye_height`, or on a child entity with a Camera component. Pitch is clamped to ±89°. JSON: `"view": "first_person"`, `"eye_height"`. How the rider looks about is set by **Look** (see below).
- **2D top-down: Face mouse.** A top-down Player Controller can tick **Face mouse**. While riding, the sprite turns toward the mouse cursor (in world space, under the camera) or along a gamepad's right stick. Draw the art facing up. Only the drawn sprite turns; the collider keeps its authored rotation, so walls don't catch a turning player. The Empty 2D Top-down template has it on. JSON: `"face_mouse": true` in `"player"` (left out when off, so older files are unchanged). Platformer riders ignore it. The aim is local and isn't sent over the network.
- **3D Camera component.** An entity with `"camera": { "fov": 60 }` is the Play camera when there is no player and no VR. In first person, a child camera of the player is the eye.

## Mouse look (3D Player)

The 3D Player component looks about with the mouse by default. Pick the mode under **Look** in the Player inspector:

| Look | First person | Third person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mouse** (default) | The mouse turns the body (yaw) and tilts the eyes (pitch, ±89°). WASD walks where you look. | The mouse orbits the camera round the rider (yaw, and pitch from 70° above to 30° below). The scroll wheel zooms. WASD walks relative to the camera and the rider turns to face where it walks. |
| **Keys** | Q / Z turn, Page Up / Page Down look up and down, right-drag does both. The cursor is left alone. | Q / Z or right-drag orbit. |

Q / Z, Page Up / Page Down and a gamepad's right stick (150°/s) work in both modes, so the keyboard is always a fallback.

With **Mouse** you also get these settings:

- **Sensitivity**: degrees per mouse count (default 0.15).
- **Invert Y**: pushing the mouse (or stick) up looks down.
- **Smoothing**: off by default. Up to 0.9 eases the view toward the mouse each tick.
- **Capture cursor on Play** (on by default): hides and locks the cursor while riding, using raw mouse motion where the platform has it. With it off, the free cursor looks about as it moves over the view.

**Taking the cursor back.**
- *Editor Play (3D):* Play captures the cursor in the viewport and ImGui ignores the mouse while it's captured. The first **Esc** frees the cursor and the next one stops Play. Losing focus, ticking **Pause** or **Free cam (C)**, or an in-game menu also frees it. Click the view to look again; that click isn't passed on as a throw. The status bar says which state you're in.
- *ts_game:* the cursor is captured from the start. **Esc** opens the pause menu and frees it, and so does losing focus. Resuming, or clicking in the window, captures it again; that click isn't a grab.

**Third-person camera collision.** The orbit camera is pulled in front of any static collider (box or capsule bounds) between the rider and the camera. It never gets closer than 0.8 m.

**scene.json.** All fields are optional, so the scene version stays at 16. A rider with no `"look"` looks with the mouse. Only values that differ from the defaults are written: `"look": "keys"`, `"mouse_sensitivity"`, `"invert_y": true`, `"look_smoothing"` and `"capture_cursor": false`.

**Templates and samples.** Empty 3D First-person and Empty 3D Third-person ship with mouse look, and so does every sample with a 3D Player (Ghost Town 3D). VR is unchanged: the headset looks. A VR rig riding flat on the desktop (no headset) uses Keys look, so the cursor stays free.

**Turning it off for a claim.** Select the Player and set **Look** to **Keys**, or untick **Capture cursor on Play** to keep mouse look with a free cursor. In `scene.json`, add `"look": "keys"` to the player object.

## Plugins per project

Turn plugins on or off for a project in the **Outfitter's Wagon** (the Plugins panel, `src/editor/plugins/PluginsPanel.*`). Turning one on brings its dependencies along, and turning one off also turns off its dependents. The list is saved to `project.json` right away. `ts_game`, `ts_server` and `ts_client` load the project's plugins too.

Built-in plugins (`src/plugins/`):

| Plugin | Default | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `ts.posse` | on | Multiplayer |
| `ts.tumbleweed` | off | Sample gameplay plugin |
| `ts.frontier` | off | Survival layer, `src/plugins/survival` |
| `ts.shipyard` | off | Airship Works ship builder, `src/plugins/shipyard` |
| `sample.lantern` | n/a | Sample dynamic plugin |
| `ts.physics.jolt` | off | Jolt Physics (3D / VR), `src/plugins/physics_jolt` |
| `ts.physics.box2d` | off | Box2D Physics (2D), `src/plugins/physics_box2d` |
| `ts.move.rider3d` | off | Rider 3D: first / third person and mouse look, `src/plugins/rider3d` |
| `ts.move.topdown` | off | Top-down Rider (2D), `src/plugins/movement2d` |
| `ts.move.platformer` | off | Platformer Rider (2D), `src/plugins/movement2d` |

The physics and movement plugins are switched on by the templates and claims that use them, and claims from before 0.2.0 get them on first open (with a backup). See [physics.md](physics.md).

The root README documents Frontier Survival and Airship Works in depth.
